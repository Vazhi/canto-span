# Source review addendum: null-object classes, lexical valency, and `V咗喇`

## Purpose

This addendum sharpens the pre-audit analysis for issue #444. It separates what the checked literature directly supports from hypotheses that still require the frozen survey audit and controlled judgments.

## 1. Lee (2018): object omission is not one category

Lee's Cantonese study distinguishes ordinary specific anaphoric object drop from three anaphoric non-specific subtypes:

1. existential;
2. generic;
3. attributive.

The proposed derivation is NP ellipsis licensed by an antecedent, with a null determiner remaining. The source therefore supports **multiple interpretations of an unpronounced object**, not a single generic `pro` or one universal hidden lexical NP.

For issue #444, the immediate consequence is that the same objectless surface may require different coding depending on discourse:

| Contextual profile | Candidate analysis | What must be shown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One salient previously mentioned entity | specific anaphoric object omission | interpretation and antecedent recovery are stable |
| A previously introduced but non-specific set/type | existential, generic, or attributive NP ellipsis | the subtype is distinguishable from an event-only reading |
| No antecedent and no specific patient intended | lexical/constructional event-only or conventional-activity reading | omission cannot simply be assumed from the surface |
| No antecedent but hearer supplies a conventional patient | inferential or culturally conventional recovery | recovery is reproducible and verb-sensitive |

Lee does not test the exact `V咗喇` combinations in the pilot. His analysis supplies a classification space, not a final parse for those items.

## 2. Perfective `咗` and sentence-final `喇` are independent layers

The checked aspect sources classify postverbal `咗` as perfective. They do not license the inference that an omitted object exists, that the event necessarily culminated, or that every affected participant is recoverable.

Sybesma and Li (2007) analyze `laa3` as a realization-of-state particle, approximately contributing that a state of affairs now holds or has become relevant. They explicitly discuss `laa3` with completed events and with actions about to begin. This makes `喇` a discourse/update layer that can combine with different predicate and argument-structure profiles.

Accordingly, `V咗喇` must be decomposed analytically into at least:

```text
[verb and its lexical/constructional valency]
+ [perfective 咗]
+ [sentence-final 喇]
+ [discourse context]
```

No one layer determines the others.

## 3. Natural objectless activity does not settle whether an object is syntactically present

Lai and Pang (2023) discuss a Cantonese resultative example `食飽先有力減肥`, noting that no specific food is implied even though `食` is treated as transitive in the cited descriptive tradition. Their point is theoretical and concerns how argument structure is represented in a larger construction.

For issue #444, the example is useful as a **boundary case**: a surface with no overt patient may be natural even where a lexical analysis expects a patient role. It does not by itself decide among a contextually inferable object, a construction-level participant, a conventional activity reading, or a different lexical sense.

## 4. Pre-audit classification rule

Until controlled evidence is available, objectless `V咗喇` tokens should be coded with separate fields rather than a single hidden-object label:

- overt antecedent present: yes/no/uncertain;
- antecedent specificity: specific/non-specific/generic/none;
- verb class and lexical sense;
- conventional activity reading available: yes/no/uncertain;
- event-only paraphrase accepted: yes/no/uncertain;
- object-recovery paraphrase accepted: yes/no/uncertain;
- role of `喇`: change/update/current relevance/other/uncertain;
- fragment or full assertion status;
- source type and context span.

## 5. Updated working conclusion

The checked sources support all of the following as live possibilities:

1. discourse-anaphoric object omission;
2. non-specific NP ellipsis;
3. conventional or generic activity readings;
4. lexical or construction-level valency alternation;
5. a full assertion with no uniquely recoverable object;
6. independent perfective and sentence-final-particle contributions.

They do **not** support a universal parser action that inserts one hidden object into every objectless `V咗喇` clause. The final issue decision remains blocked on the frozen survey audit, verb-class contrasts, and context-bearing corpus evidence.
