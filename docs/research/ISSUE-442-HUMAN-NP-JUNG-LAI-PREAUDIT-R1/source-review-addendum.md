# Source review addendum: coverb structure and the human-NP ambiguity

## Purpose

This addendum records a deeper full-text review of Francis and Matthews (2006) and clarifies how its coverb analysis constrains issue #442. It supplements the main pre-audit note; it does not replace the final frozen survey audit.

## Francis and Matthews (2006): verified findings

Francis and Matthews analyze Cantonese coverb serial constructions as clauses of the form:

```text
subject + V1 + NP1 + V2 (+ NP2)
```

The first verb and its following noun phrase express relations such as location, direction, instrument, beneficiary, accompaniment, or related circumstances. The V1 phrase precedes and semantically modifies the main predicate V2.

Their full-text judgment experiment used 40 participants and manipulated coverb verb, object extraction, and aspect marking. The tested coverbs remained highly acceptable with aspect marking in ordinary two-verb clauses, supporting verbal rather than prepositional status. The authors nevertheless treat V2 as the semantic head of the coverb construction regardless of where aspect is marked. They also report substantial verb-specific variability, cautioning against deriving one categorical syntactic rule from a surface sequence alone.

## Consequence for `Human NP + 用嚟 + VP`

The paper does not test conventionalized `用嚟` directly, but its argument-structure template supplies an important negative diagnostic.

For a string such as:

```text
阿明用嚟切方包
```

an analysis in which `阿明` is the ordinary agent/user and `用` is a coverb-like verb would normally leave the entity used by `用` unexpressed. That analysis is therefore an **omitted-resource analysis**, not evidence that the human NP itself is the instrument or resource.

Conversely, a direct human-resource analysis would require independent evidence that a human-denoting NP can occupy the resource/instrument position of the conventionalized `用嚟` relation. None of the currently checked construction descriptions supplies that evidence.

The coverb source therefore sharpens, but does not settle, the pilot ambiguity:

| Surface interpretation | What the source contributes | Remaining requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human NP is the user/agent; resource omitted | Structurally plausible as a missing V1 object or discourse-recovered resource | Controlled contexts must show whether speakers actually recover a resource and which antecedents license it |
| Human NP is the direct resource/instrument | Not established | Matched human/nonhuman contrasts and independent natural attestations are required |
| Human NP is a topic or assignee | Not analyzed by this source | Topic, assignment, and responsibility structures must be tested separately |
| String is rejected or repaired | Compatible with failure to recover the missing resource or with semantic mismatch | Reasons and corrections must be adjudicated after survey freeze |

## Updated source status

`SRC-FRANCIS-MATTHEWS-2006` is now treated as **author-uploaded full text reopened**, not merely metadata/abstract. Its claim weight remains indirect: it supports the coverb argument-structure and headedness boundary but does not establish productive human-resource `用嚟`.

## Pre-audit working conclusion

The source record currently favors a conservative split:

1. nonhuman instrument/resource `NP + 用嚟 + VP` has direct descriptive support;
2. resource-topic + overt human user + `用嚟 + VP` has direct attestation;
3. isolated human-initial strings can be compatible with omitted-resource or topic analyses;
4. a productive bare human-resource construction is not yet source-supported.

This conclusion is provisional and source-limited. It cannot be converted into a construction identity, status decision, parser rule, or final survey interpretation before the frozen audit and controlled evidence are complete.
