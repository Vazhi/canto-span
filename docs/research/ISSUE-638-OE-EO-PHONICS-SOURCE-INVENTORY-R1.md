## Official-final conclusions

The reviewed sources support this stable inventory:

```text
oe   [œː]
oeng [œːŋ]
oet  [œːt̚]
oek  [œːk̚]

eoi  [ɵɥ]
eon  [ɵn]
eot  [ɵt̚]

yu   [yː]
yun  [yːn]
yut  [yːt̚]
```

Narrow phonetic realizations may vary by analysis and speaker. The directly inspected scholarly locator supplies `[ɵɥ]`; the terminal conclusion requires only that `eoi` is diphthongal and distinct from `yun`.

## Claims not supported by the evidence

The reviewed sources do not support:

- that [œ] and [ɵ] are unique to Cantonese;
- that `oe` occurs only before `-ng` and `-k`;
- that `eoi` is a plain monophthong;
- that `jyun4` belongs to the `eo` final row;
- that any source pair changes only the vowel;
- that I097 has a recoverable intended partner;
- that I094’s and I096’s combined glosses belong wholly to the displayed tones;
- that source preservation authorizes silent correction;
- that a parser or construction identity is involved.
